=== MISSION ===
You are hunting for backtest delusions - places where the backtest is unrealistically optimistic compared to real trading.

=== GOAL ===
Find every assumption that makes backtests look better than real trading would be. Overfitting to backtests is how trading systems blow up in production.

Your success is measured by: Finding unrealistic assumptions that would cause live trading to underperform backtests.

=== OUTPUT REQUIREMENT ===
Write your complete analysis to: c:\fast_swarm\swarm\botresearch\backtest_realism.md

=== MINDSET ===
Real trading is WORSE than backtests because:
- You can't execute at exact prices (slippage)
- Every trade has fees
- Large orders move the market (impact)
- You can't always get filled (liquidity)
- Bid-ask spread costs money

=== QUESTIONS TO ANSWER ===
- Where does entry price come from? (should be NEXT candle open, not current close)
- Are fees subtracted from PnL?
- Is there any slippage model?
- What happens with large position sizes?
- Are we assuming we can always enter/exit instantly?

=== OPTIMISM DETECTORS ===
- entry_price = candle["close"]  <- OPTIMISTIC (can't execute at close)
- pnl = (exit - entry) / entry   <- OPTIMISTIC (no fees)
- No slippage parameter anywhere <- OPTIMISTIC
